## Problem: Imbalanced routing (Continued)
The problem was illustrated in [the last post](https://robinc.vercel.app/post.html?id=011-MoE1), Italics are directly quoted from the paper.

### Solution: EC (Expert Choice)
The authors of the [2025 ICLR paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15664) indicated that this approach *breaks the causal constraint* (causing a leakage of future information which *destroys the generalization of a model and prevents reliable evaluation*). They support their hypothesis (*that the loss drop originates from the model’s accessing and exploiting future token information*) with experiments.

### Solution: Loss-Free Balancing
We adjust each expert’s bias $b_i$ online to enforce perfectly balanced routing. Algorithm 1 (Wang et al., 2025) shows how:

**Algorithm 1** (Wang et al., 2025): adjusting the per-expert bias $b_i$ during training.
Input: MoE model $\theta$, batch iterator $\mathcal{B}$, bias-update rate $u$.

1. Initialize $b_i \leftarrow 0$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.
2. For each batch $\{(x_k, y_k)\}_k \in \mathcal{B}$:
    - Compute the raw gating scores $s_{i,t} = G(\mathbf{u}_t^\top e_i)$.
    - Prune with the bias:
      $$
      g_{i,t} =
      \begin{cases}
        s_{i,t}, & s_{i,t} + b_i \in \text{Topk}(\{s_{j,t} + b_j \mid 1 \leq j \leq N\}, K) \\
        0, & \text{otherwise}
      \end{cases}
      $$
      The bias only takes part in the top-$K$ selection; the weight $g_{i,t}$ is still the unbiased score.
    - Train $\theta$ on this batch using the weights $g_{i,t}$.
    - Count the assignments and the load error:
      $$
      c_i = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{1}(\text{token } t \text{ selects expert } i), \qquad \bar c = \frac{KT}{N}, \qquad e_i = \bar c - c_i .
      $$
    - Update the bias: $b_i \leftarrow b_i + u \cdot \sgn(e_i)$.
3. Return $\theta$ and the $b_i$.

The paper reuses two letters here: $u$ is the update rate, not a token's input $\mathbf{u}_t$, and $e_i$ is the load error, not the expert centroid from part 1.

No extra loss term is needed. Each step nudges $b_i$ up for an underloaded expert and down for an overloaded one, which pushes every $c_i$ toward the balanced count $\bar c = KT/N$; the paper demonstrates the resulting balance experimentally.
