## Setup

- **Hypothesis set / space:** $\mathcal H$
- **Machine (model):** $M : \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^{d'}$
- **Reference machine:** $M^\star$  
  (the “ground-truth” rule we want to match)

Sometimes we imagine the ideal condition
$$
M(x) = M^\star(x)\quad \forall x\in \mathbb R^d.
$$

## Distance / loss

A loss (called “distance” in the notes) is a function
$$
\Delta : \mathbb R^{d'} \times \mathcal H \times \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R_{+}.
$$

Example (0–1 loss):
$$
\Delta(y, M, x) =
\begin{cases}
0, & \text{if } y = M(x),\\
1, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$
(annotated as “cost”)

## Ideal minimization objective

Choose a model to minimize population cost:
$$
\arg\min_{M\in\mathcal H}\; \int_{\mathbb R^d} \Delta\!\big(M^\star(x),\, M,\, x\big)\,dx.
$$

**Issues noted:**
1. Can we compute this integral?
2. Is there an $M$ that minimizes this?
3. Do we want to do this over the entire input space?

## Manifold / data-distribution assumption

Assume inputs are distributed as $x\sim p(x)$. Then:
$$
\arg\min_{M\in\mathcal H}\; \int_{\mathbb R^d} p(x)\,\Delta\!\big(M^\star(x),\, M,\, x\big)\,dx
\;=\;
\arg\min_{M\in\mathcal H}\; \mathbb E_x\!\left[\Delta\!\big(M^\star(x),\, M,\, x\big)\right].
$$
(annotated as “expected cost”)

---

## Monte Carlo approximation (empirical cost)

Sample
$$
x_1,x_2,\dots,x_N \sim p(x).
$$
Then
$$
\mathbb E_x\!\left[\Delta\!\big(M^\star(x),\, M,\, x\big)\right]
\approx
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N \Delta\!\big(M^\star(x_n),\, M,\, x_n\big).
$$

If labels are provided as
$$
y_n = M^\star(x_n),
$$
then the empirical objective becomes
$$
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N \Delta(y_n, M, x_n).
$$

### Empirical risk minimization over the hypothesis space
$$
\arg\min_{M\in\mathcal H}\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N \Delta(y_n, M, x_n).
$$

## Regularization

Add a regularizer $C(M)$ with a positive constant $\lambda$:
$$
\arg\min_{M\in\mathcal H}\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N \Delta(y_n, M, x_n) \;+\; \lambda\,C(M).
$$
(annotated as “regularization cost / extra constraints”)

Define the training objective
$$
J(M, D_{\text{train}}),
\qquad
D_{\text{train}}=\{(x_1,y_1), (x_2,y_2), \dots, (x_N,y_N)\}.
$$

---

## Inference (weighting models by training objective)

- Score each $M$ according to $J$.
- Define a Gibbs-style weight:
$$
w(M) = \frac{1}{Z}\exp\!\left\{-J(M, D_{\text{train}})\right\},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{M'\in\mathcal H}\exp\!\left\{-J(M', D_{\text{train}})\right\}.
$$

- Predict by model averaging:
$$
\text{output}(x) = \sum_{M\in\mathcal H} w(M)\,M(x).
$$

More generally, with inverse-temperature $\beta$:
$$
w_\beta(M) = \frac{1}{Z_\beta}\exp\!\left\{-\beta\,J(M, D_{\text{train}})\right\}.
$$

As $\beta\to\infty$,
$$
\sum_{M\in\mathcal H} w_\beta(M)\,M(x) \to \hat M(x),
\qquad
\hat M = \arg\min_{M\in\mathcal H} J(M, D_{\text{train}}).
$$
